## Concrete findings

### NP01 — P1: unchanged cold startup invalidates durable queued work

`app/src/main/java/com/halo/app/di/ManualDependencyGraph.kt:170–175` subscribes to Kids state and unconditionally invokes `policy.invalidateAuthority()` for every emission, including the initial StateFlow value and asynchronous readiness. `KidsPreferences.kt:16–29` begins unready and loads asynchronously. `PrivacyModeStore.kt:121` increments the persisted revision when invalidating. Meanwhile `LiteRtDownloadWorker.kt:19–22` restores the revision captured in its WorkManager input.

A Power download queued before process death therefore sees a new policy revision when the next process creates the same graph, even when durable mode and adult permissions are unchanged. Depending on startup ordering it is blocked immediately, cancelled after starting, or receives a revision that becomes stale after the readiness emission. This is a predictable restoration failure, not proof of a user's revocation.

Preserve fail-closed readiness, but distinguish establishing the restored authority snapshot from a real authority change. Await readiness before admission; do not silently refresh old work to arbitrary new authority. A process-local Kids sharing grant that genuinely expired must still block.

Acceptance: queue in adult Power, kill/recreate the process with unchanged settings, and prove the same authorized job can proceed once readiness is established. Separately prove changed mode/profile and lost Kids grant stay blocked with a purposeful recovery outcome.

### NP02 — P1: stale offline queue can swallow an explicit Resume

`providers/litert/.../LiteRtModelManager.kt:327–330` marks a request Queued and enqueues unique work with `KEEP`, including while Local is active. Policy transitions revoke active HTTP leases, but no reviewed mode-change owner cancels ENQUEUED/BLOCKED LiteRT work. Its network constraint can keep it queued indefinitely. `resumeDownload` at 352 goes through the same `KEEP` enqueue path; `isDownloading` also treats that stale queue as busy.

Concrete sequence: queue in Power while offline; switch Local, then Power; explicitly resume the same model before connectivity returns. The old unfinished job keeps its stale revision, so WorkManager can discard the fresh request. When connectivity returns the old job fails its policy check instead of honoring the new explicit Resume. Before that it may continue to say Queued in Local rather than paused by policy.

Cancel/pause stale pending acquisition on authority change and make explicit resume replace or await cancellation of the stale job without cancelling independently owned work or discarding partial bytes. Avoid creating misleading network work while authority is already unavailable. Preserve local import and verification.

Acceptance: the offline sequence above, with deterministic cancellation/enqueue ordering, then reconnect. Exactly the newly authorized intent may request bytes. Also test a Local download attempt, mode change while an active body streams, and ownership separation between setup and independently started work.

### NP03 — P1: a multi-stage setup can adopt fresh authority without Resume

`app/src/main/java/com/halo/app/ai/LocalRolePackInstaller.kt:346–360` launches the accepted operation without a captured policy revision or authority watcher. `installTransaction` at 435–489 advances through intent, Whisper, VAD and Supertonic. The called owners independently capture the current revision: `LiteRtModelManager.kt:329/340`, `SpeechModelInventoryRepository.kt:103`, and `SupertonicPackStore.kt:159`.

If Local→Power occurs while the setup is hashing, inspecting installed files or extracting a completed archive between network steps, no active HTTP lease exists to cancel. The next stage captures the new Power revision and starts from the old setup request. The same issue exists for delayed preflight before the first stage. The single-request fence is therefore insufficient for the accepted multi-stage operation.

Capture and fence one revision for each explicit setup start/resume (including the optional conversation extension), or cancel/pause the entire operation on authority change. Pass that authority through every child acquisition. Preserve verified files and the ownership journal so a deliberate Resume can continue them; neither installation nor cancellation should select or qualify a model.

Acceptance: pause at a local preflight or inter-stage verification latch, switch Power→Local→Power, release it, and assert zero requests by the old operation. An explicit Resume may then acquire the new revision and use preserved files. Test tool-originated setup as well as the manual route.

### NP04 — P2: policy blocks are presented as connection/storage failures

`RemoteDispatchBlocked` is an IOException. `providers/litert/.../LiteRtModelDownloader.kt:349–373` converts initial policy denial into NETWORK with “Check your connection”; mid-request revocation is merely generic cancellation. `KokoroSpeechProvider.kt:77–79` presents a retry failure, while `KokoroPackControls.kt:31/89` discards the exception reason. `LocalRolePackInstaller.kt:806–810` tells users to check storage/connection. The direct Supertonic setup catch in `HaloSetupScreen.kt:278` does the same. These download controls remain enabled without explaining Local or missing parental authority.

A user in deliberate Local mode can repeatedly retry on a healthy connection and never discover the actual recovery step. Raw “current mode profile” text in the other voice owner also lacks a specific action and does not distinguish an authority change from missing permissions.

Expose a typed blocked/paused outcome from the policy boundary and preserve it through worker output, setup journal and UI. Explain the applicable mode/profile/parent restriction and how to resume after it changes, using the canonical Local/Power names. Keep Android/account failures distinct and keep imports/installed local use available. Do not add a new consent dialog for ordinary requests already authorized by Power.

Acceptance: Local attempt, active revocation, stale queued revision and parent denial each show their actual reason and usable next action across LiteRT, Kokoro, Supertonic, Sherpa and setup. Connection retry must not be the proposed fix for a policy denial. Verify localized UI once those controls are integrated.

Recheck authoritative current revision immediately before cancellation (and avoid publishing a stale blocked result). Test a deliberately delayed revision-flow delivery followed by new-revision enqueue; current work must survive. This is separate from correctly cancelling old work.

**NP04 is partially addressed.** Initial `RemoteDispatchBlocked` now maps to `MODE_BLOCKED` in LiteRT and an explicit recovery message in the corrected voice/setup owners. Two original branches remain:

## Ordered queue and streaming follow-up

The new single Channel processor orders queue mutation and cancellation. Pending intent UUID/owner fences prevent an invalidated enqueue from running, while a fresh Resume follows the queued cancellation. This addresses the late-registration part of NP02-A. The WorkInfo observer now compares against `dispatchPolicy.revision` at its decision rather than a lagging combined emission, addressing NP02-B in source.

**NP02-A remains open for the cancelled-before-registration state.** `LiteRtModelManager.kt:355` publishes Queued before registering work. If `cancelDownload`/`cancelOwnedDownload` at 408–424 removes that pending intent, the enqueue closure returns at 364 or 371. The subsequent WorkManager cancellation finds no registered work, so it need not emit any WorkInfo transition. Nothing in this path changes the earlier Queued state; `isDownloading` remains true and can indefinitely block setup/normal recovery even though no transfer exists. Publish an owner/generation-fenced Paused or NotInstalled outcome for cancellation of an unregistered intent, without overwriting a later Resume or an independent current transfer. Acceptance must hold registration at a latch, Pause before a WorkInfo exists, release the latch, then verify no network work, a nonbusy state and a usable explicit Resume.

**NP04-A's reported streaming mechanism is addressed in source.** The public GET wrapper records policy revocation separately from user call cancellation, rethrows policy interruption from the request path, and wraps response reads so a revoked body surfaces `RemoteDispatchBlocked`. **Remaining NP02-A consequence — P2: old cancellation can overwrite newer foreground verification.** `publishQueueCancellation` at `LiteRtModelManager.kt:436–441` checks WorkManager registration and `pendingQueueIntents`, but does not check `downloadJobs`/`downloadOwners` or a foreground generation. If an older cancellation command is still queued, it can run after that new foreground verification starts, observe no registered/pending background job, and change the new Preparing/Downloading receipt to Paused while the new job is active. An old setup-owned cancellation can similarly mislabel independent foreground verification. Fence cancellation presentation against any newer or independent active foreground job as well. Preserve the old-job cancellation outcome and the existing final/policy receipts. Acceptance: delay the ordered cancellation, start complete-partial verification under a new action/owner, then release it and assert that the new receipt remains active and reaches its own outcome. This is a status-ownership defect; no network-authority or artifact-integrity bypass is asserted.
